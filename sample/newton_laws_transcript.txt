Hello everyone, and welcome to this lesson on Newton's laws of motion. Today we will look at three ideas that explain almost every movement you see around you, from a ball rolling on the ground to a rocket leaving the earth.

Let us start with the first law, which is also called the law of inertia. It says that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed, unless an unbalanced force acts on it. Think about a book lying on a table. It does not move by itself, because nothing pushes it. Now think about a passenger in a bus. When the bus suddenly brakes, the passenger leans forward. The body wants to keep moving, and that tendency to resist a change in motion is called inertia. The more mass an object has, the more inertia it has. That is why it is harder to push a loaded cart than an empty one.

Now the second law. It tells us how force, mass and acceleration are connected. The formula is force equals mass times acceleration, written as F equals m a. If you push two objects with the same force, the lighter one accelerates more. If you want the same acceleration for a heavier object, you must apply a bigger force. Force is measured in newtons. One newton is the force that gives a one kilogram object an acceleration of one metre per second squared. So if a two kilogram trolley accelerates at three metres per second squared, the force acting on it is six newtons.

Next, the third law. It says that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When you push a wall, the wall pushes back on your hand with the same strength. When a swimmer pushes the water backwards, the water pushes the swimmer forward. A rocket works in the same way. It throws hot gas downwards, and the gas pushes the rocket upwards. An important point is that the action and reaction forces act on two different objects, so they do not cancel each other out.

Finally, let us talk about friction, because it explains why things stop in real life. In everyday life a rolling ball slows down and stops, which seems to break the first law. But the ball stops only because friction between the ball and the ground is an unbalanced force acting against its motion. In space, where there is almost no friction, an object keeps moving for a very long time.

To summarise: the first law is about inertia, the second law connects force, mass and acceleration, and the third law says forces always come in pairs. Friction is the unbalanced force that makes moving objects slow down on earth. Practise with a few numerical problems on F equals m a, and try to find three examples of the third law at home. Thank you for watching, and see you in the next lesson.
